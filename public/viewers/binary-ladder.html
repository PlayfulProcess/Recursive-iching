The Binary Ladder

One bit becomes three becomes six — and then, in a system built four decades ago, sixty-four arcs of a circle. Yin and yang, the eight trigrams, the sixty-four hexagrams, the Human Design mandala: each smaller world is what you see of the larger one when you can only observe a few of its lines. Read to know yourself, not to be told your fate — relate to the hexagram, never obey it.

Level 1 — One bit: yin and yang

A single line is either broken or solid — the whole 1-dimensional world. Leibniz read broken as 0 and solid as 1. Pick a line position, then a value, and watch which trigrams below share it.

Tap a value above — it highlights every trigram in Level 2 whose line at this position agrees. That highlighted set is the 1-bit world's view of the 3-bit world: everything else about the trigram is invisible from here.

Level 2 — Three bits: the eight trigrams

Three lines, bottom to top, give 2³ = 8 combinations — the trigrams (八卦), arranged here 2×4 in Fu Xi's binary order (乾 111 = 7 down to 坤 000 = 0). Click one to see its three lines isolated, and to highlight its row and column in the hexagram matrix below.

Click a trigram to see its three lines named individually.

Level 3 — Six bits: the sixty-four hexagrams

Two trigrams stacked give 2⁶ = 64 hexagrams. Rows are the lower trigram, columns the upper — the space's native, binary coordinates. A hexagram's grid position never moves; what changes below is only which number is written in each cell.

A trigram is the trace of this 64-hexagram world restricted to three lines: select one in Level 2 and its row (as lower trigram) and column (as upper trigram) light up — every hexagram that shares those three lines with it, the rest of the six lines left free.

Click a cell to open the hexagram (both numbers, its binary vector, and its two trigrams).

Level 4 — The same sixty-four, bent onto a circle: the Human Design mandala

Human Design — a modern system, synthesised by Ra Uru Hu in 1987 out of the I Ching, astrology, Kabbalah and the chakras — calls the hexagrams gates, keeps their King Wen numbers exactly, and gives each one 5°37′30″ of the zodiac (360° ÷ 64). Click any gate here to light up its cell in the matrix above, or any cell up there to find it down here. The dashed line points at the gate 180° away — always this one's exact opposite, all six lines flipped.

Outer ring: the twelve zodiac signs, 30° each, 0° Aries at the top and longitude increasing clockwise (printed mandalas vary in orientation; nothing depends on it). Inner ring: the 64 gates in mandala order, beginning with Gate 41 at 2°00′ Aquarius. Sources and the verification behind this order are in docs/HUMAN-DESIGN-TRACE.md.

History & framing

Documented history

What Leibniz actually saw, and when

Gottfried Leibniz worked out binary arithmetic on his own starting around 1679 — base-2 rules, no contact with China yet. In 1701 the Jesuit missionary Joachim Bouvet, writing from the Kangxi court in Beijing, sent him a woodcut of the Fu Xi hexagram arrangement (in fact devised by the 11th-century philosopher Shao Yong) and suggested reading solid lines as 1, broken as 0. The letter took over a year to arrive; Leibniz received it in early 1703 and, delighted, published a note that same year in Paris arguing the ancient Chinese had anticipated his own binary system by four thousand years.

The one-liner worth keeping: his binary arithmetic predates seeing the diagram by roughly two decades. He didn't learn binary from the I Ching — he recognized (and, most historians now think, over-read) his own already-finished idea inside Shao Yong's ordering. That ordering is real and elegant — reading each hexagram's six lines bottom-to-top as a binary number does put the 64 hexagrams in exact numerical sequence 0–63, which is exactly what Level 3's "Fu Xi order" toggle shows — but no ancient Chinese text treats the hexagrams as arithmetic; that's a much later reading laid over an older object.

Documented history

Fu Xi order vs. King Wen order — a permutation, not a discovery

The King Wen sequence (the traditional 1–64 numbering used in most printed books, including this repo's own Books viewer) groups hexagrams by paired relationships — inversion and alternation — with no binary logic of its own. Toggle Level 3 to "King Wen number" and watch the numbers scatter across the same fixed grid: same 64 objects, same 64 positions, just a different one-to-one relabeling. That relabeling — a bijection from one ordering onto another — is what a mathematician means by a permutation, and this is a clean, honest place to see one.

The third toggle, Mandala slot, adds a third such relabeling — position around Level 4's circle, counting from Gate 41. Three orderings of one set of 64 objects, each a permutation of the others: a counting order (Fu Xi), a meaning order (King Wen), and a positional order (the mandala). The objects never move; only the labels do.

Documented, modern

Human Design: whose system this is, and what its wheel order actually is

Level 4 draws a modern system faithfully, without adopting it. Human Design was originated by Alan Robert Krakower, who took the name Ra Uru Hu and published the system in 1992 after an experience in January 1987 on Ibiza he described as an eight-day encounter with a "Voice." It is a single-author synthesis of the I Ching, Western astrology, the Kabbalistic Tree of Life and the chakra system; Wikipedia classifies it as pseudoscience. It is roughly forty years old, it does not claim to be ancient, and this page makes no claim about whether any of it works. What it does have — and the only reason it appears here — is an interesting piece of combinatorics.

Human Design keeps the King Wen numbers exactly (its Gate n is hexagram n) but discards the King Wen sequence, giving each gate 5°37′30″ of the ecliptic in a different order beginning with Gate 41 at 2°00′ Aquarius. Practitioner sources often say the wheel "follows the Fu Xi sequence." That is half right, and the precise version is prettier: walking forward from Gate 2 (all yin, 13°15′ Taurus), the next thirty-two gates are the Fu Xi binary count 0, 1, 2 … 31 — then the wheel jumps to Gate 1 (all yang, 13°15′ Scorpio) and counts back down 63 … 32. The consequence, true for all thirty-two pairs without exception: the gate directly opposite any gate is its exact binary complement, every line flipped — which is the dashed axis Level 4 draws. Human Design calls those pairs programming partners; a Sun and its Earth always form one.

So the mandala is not an arbitrary shuffle. Complementation already partitions the 64 hexagrams into 32 opposite pairs — a structure the circle can hold — and this ordering is an arrangement that respects it. That much is checkable arithmetic, and it was checked here against three independent sources for the gate order (two published degree tables and an open-source chart library) before this level shipped. The working, the sources, and what was deliberately left unclaimed are in docs/HUMAN-DESIGN-TRACE.md. What is not derivable from the hexagrams: the nine centres and thirty-six channels of the body graph. Only three of those thirty-six gate pairings are binary complements and the rest show no pattern at all — that layer is added, not hidden inside the I Ching.

Modern analogy — not ancient doctrine

"Trace": a teaching analogy borrowed from current consciousness research

Cognitive scientist Donald Hoffman (UC Irvine) and coauthors have a speculative, math-heavy research program — published mostly in Frontiers in Psychology and Entropy, with a 2024 piece still only a preprint — that models reality's substrate as networks of interacting Markov chains and defines a precise operation they call a trace: restrict a large chain to the states one observer can actually reach, and there's a unique smaller "trace chain" describing the dynamics as seen from inside that restriction. Their own further claim — that this asymptotically reproduces relativity, quantum theory, and the Standard Model — is explicitly a conjecture awaiting a proposed empirical test, not a result, and their broader claim that consciousness (not spacetime) is reality's base layer is a minority position even within this research area.

What this page borrows is only the shape of that idea, not Hoffman's actual mathematics: a trigram, observed as a restriction of the six-line hexagram world to three lines, and a single line, observed as a restriction of the trigram world to one — "what a smaller world looks like from inside a bigger one" — is structurally the same move as Hoffman's trace (big object → restrict to what's observable → get a smaller, well-defined object), using ordinary bit vectors instead of his Markov chains. It's offered here as a thinking tool for noticing that structure, in keeping with this project's own stance: a mirror for understanding, never a claim about how reality actually works.

Level 4 is that same move once more, one rung further out. A Human Design chart is a much larger labelled object: not 64 states but which of 64 arcs and which sixth of that arc and which finer subdivision again, for each of thirteen celestial bodies, at each of two moments, plus a nine-node graph laid over the result. Forget every one of those coordinates except "which of the 64 arcs" and you land back on exactly the bare hexagram — the same 64 objects the other three levels are built from. That is a real projection, and it is the honest content of the phrase the I Ching as a trace of Human Design: the hexagram is what survives of the mandala when the only thing an observer can see is which arc they are in.

And here is the inversion that keeps it honest. This ladder ascends — one bit, three, six, then the wheel — but the history descends. Human Design was assembled in 1987 out of the I Ching, so the larger structure is the younger one; the hexagrams did not come from it, it came from them. Three more places the analogy gives out: a trace is defined on a Markov chain and the mandala is a static labelling, not a dynamics (for a genuine transition matrix over these 64 states, use this repo's Caster and the real casting probabilities); Hoffman's trace chain is unique given a set of observable states, while "forget the degrees" is just one projection among many; and the added layers are additions, not hidden depth — the body graph is not secretly inside the hexagrams. Take Level 4 as a picture of what restriction feels like, not as a claim that either system is true, and least of all as a claim that the older book is a shadow of the newer one.
